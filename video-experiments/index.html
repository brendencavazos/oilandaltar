In Passing — four treatments

Four ways to present the eleven clips, built against the real files. Nothing here touches the site.

Open them on your phone as well as the desktop. Video behaves differently there — autoplay rules, data, and battery — and that is where the choice is really made.

Clips
11 — nine landscape, two shot vertically
Total weight
31 MB, averaging 2.8 MB each
Titles
real ones, unlike the stills — they carry meaning
  1. 5
    Contact sheet → cinema

    The blend: the section stays a quiet grid of stills, and a chosen clip gets the whole screen on black — with arrows, swipe and keyboard.

    Cheap like 1, immersive like 3. Sound is on from the first tap where the browser allows it. Vertical clips keep their shape.

  2. 1
    Contact sheet

    Still frames in a grid like the photographic sections. Click one for sound and full screen. Nothing moves until asked.

    Cheapest: a visitor loads the one clip they chose, not 31 MB. Most consistent with the rest of the site, least arresting.

  3. 2
    Living wall

    Everything up front and alive — the same grid, but every clip plays silently and loops while on screen.

    The most arresting and the most expensive. Only what is on screen plays, but on cellular this is real data.

  4. 3
    Cinema

    One clip per screen, filling it, snapping from one to the next. Plays silently on arrival; a button turns sound on.

    Closest to what your statement describes. Nothing competes. Eleven screens is a long scroll — that is the cost.

  5. 4
    Viewing room

    One clip playing large with the rest as a strip of stills beneath. Choosing one swaps it into the stage.

    Everything reachable without scrolling, one clip loaded at a time. The strip is awkward to place on a phone.
