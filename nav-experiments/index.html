Navigation options

Four ways the navigation could work, each shown on a full-bleed opening frame that scrolls into a wall of photographs — so you can judge it both over a picture and over the white page.

All four move navigation out of the left column and across the top. That is what solves the two problems: nothing sits beside the work where it can be covered, and nothing reappears over a photograph as you scroll.

Open each on your phone. That is where the current navigation struggles, so that is where these should be judged.

  1. A
    Top bar, always there

    Wordmark left, sections right. Transparent over the opening frame, then it takes the page tone once you scroll past it.

    Everything reachable in one tap. Six sections is a lot of words across a phone — the most likely thing to feel tight.

  2. B
    Top bar with a projects panel

    The reference site's arrangement. The documentary work gathers behind PROJECTS, which drops a small panel; everything else stays in the bar.

    Keeps the bar short as the work grows. Bible Belt costs two taps.

  3. C
    Wordmark and Menu only

    Two words at the top; the whole navigation opens as a full screen when asked for.

    The quietest — nothing competes with the photographs at any point. Every section is one click further away, which matters for someone browsing rather than looking for something.

  4. D
    Top bar that gets out of the way

    Option A, but it slides away as you scroll down and returns the moment you scroll up.

    While you are looking there is nothing on screen; the instant you want to leave, it is back. Same behaviour the site's left column has now, moved to the top.
