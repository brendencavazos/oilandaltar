Portraits — six layouts

E and F group by person. The section becomes eleven sessions rather than forty-three photographs — one cover each, with the name and date, and you click into a shoot. That is the answer to the long-scroll problem.

A, B and C are flat three-column layouts. D is a single centred column, one photograph per row at its own proportions.

Every one of them reserves each frame's true shape before the file arrives, so the page never jumps as photographs load — the technique worth keeping from the reference.

The three-column options differ on What they differ on is the one thing only you can decide: whether the session headings stay.

That matters because this section's own statement is "a collaboration with a specific person, named and dated" — the names are the point, and they are the only thing Portraits has that no other section does.

  1. E
    Session index, three columns

    Eleven covers in a three-column grid, each with a name and date. Click a person to open their shoot as its own three-column wall.

    The whole section fits on one screen. Nobody scrolls past someone they were not looking for.

  2. F
    Session index, single column

    The same eleven sessions, but each person announced at full width, one per row. Their shoot opens as a single centred column.

    Grander and slower — eleven screens instead of one, but far shorter than all forty-three.

  3. D
    Single centred column

    One photograph per row, centred, nothing cropped and nothing sharing a screen. A grey box holds each frame's exact shape before it loads, so the page never jumps.

    Treats a portrait as something to look at rather than scan. A long page — 43 photographs — which is the trade.

  4. A
    Flat three-column wall

    The same mosaic as every other section. No headings; the name appears when a frame is opened.

    Most consistent with the site, and lopsided sessions stop mattering. The named-collaboration idea leaves the page.

  5. B
    Sessions, three columns each

    Headings stay — each shoot runs as its own three-column group beneath its name.

    Honours what the section says it is. Four headings will sit over a single photograph.

  6. C
    Uniform three-column grid

    Equal 2:3 cells, name and date beneath each frame. Closest to the reference you sent.

    Reads as a roster of people rather than a wall of pictures. Keeps every name visible without a heading over one photograph.
